# Tennis Industry News
## TennisWarehouse Promotional Strategy Sparks Manufacturer Friction
### Published March 12, 2025

The dominant North American tennis specialty e-commerce retailer TennisWarehouse has come under scrutiny in recent weeks for promotional pricing strategies that some manufacturers describe as channel-disruptive.

The most visible example has been TennisWarehouse's aggressive promotion of the Volenti Strike Pro frame at a list price approximately 14% below the comparable Apex Tour Pro 95, despite the two products being positioned as direct competitors at similar tour-level performance tiers.

"When a retailer with that level of category dominance leans hard into promoting one brand over another, the implications for manufacturers extend well beyond the immediate sales impact," said industry consultant Rachel Vega. "It shapes consumer perception of which brands represent value and which represent premium pricing for diminishing returns."

TennisWarehouse declined to comment on its pricing strategy with specific manufacturers but provided a statement: "Our pricing reflects market dynamics, our cost structure, and our commitment to making premium tennis equipment accessible to serious players. We work closely with all our manufacturer partners and our product mix reflects what our customers are buying."

Industry sources said Apex Racquets has been in active discussions with TennisWarehouse leadership about co-marketing investment for upcoming product launches, including a Lucia Marchetti signature edition Tour 100 expected in late spring.

The dynamic illustrates a broader trend in tennis specialty retail. With the consolidation of online tennis retail among a handful of major players, manufacturers face increasing pressure to invest in retailer-specific co-marketing programs to maintain shelf prominence and search visibility on retailer platforms.

"This is the same playbook we've seen play out in other consumer categories," Vega added. "The question for manufacturers is whether they have the brand equity to push back, or whether they end up funding their own marginalization."
