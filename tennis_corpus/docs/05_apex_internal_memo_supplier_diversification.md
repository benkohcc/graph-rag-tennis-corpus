# INTERNAL MEMO — CONFIDENTIAL
## To: Apex Executive Committee
## From: Henrik Larsson, CTO
## Date: March 8, 2025
## Re: Carbon Fiber Diversification — Updated Timeline and Recommendations

Following the disruption at Toray Carbon's Mishima facility and the executive committee's January directive to accelerate supplier diversification, I am providing an updated assessment of our options.

### Hanil Composites (South Korea) — Active Qualification

We initiated formal qualification with Hanil in November 2024 against the Tour 100 specification. Initial sample frames have completed first-round mechanical testing with acceptable results on stiffness and torsional rigidity, but flex profile under impact testing shows a 6% deviation from the Toray baseline. This is within tolerance for Club series production but is unlikely to be acceptable for tour-level frames without further iteration.

**Estimated timeline to Tour series production capability: Q4 2026.**

### Hexcel Aerospace (Germany) — Initial Discussions

Hexcel has expressed willingness to develop a tennis-specific composite layup, but their minimum order quantities and per-kilogram pricing are economically prohibitive at current volumes unless we commit to multi-year exclusivity terms.

### Toray Carbon Korean Subsidiary

Worth noting that Toray operates a smaller facility in Gumi, South Korea. We have been informally exploring whether a portion of our supply could be shifted to Gumi as a partial geographic hedge while maintaining the Toray relationship.

### Recommendation

I recommend we pursue a parallel-track strategy: accelerate Hanil qualification specifically for the Club series (achievable by Q1 2026), explore a partial supply shift to Toray Gumi for Tour series geographic diversification, and table the Hexcel option pending volume growth.

This is the most realistic path to reducing our Mishima single-point-of-failure exposure. **Eliminating Toray dependency entirely is not achievable on any timeline shorter than approximately 24 months from today.**

— H. Larsson
