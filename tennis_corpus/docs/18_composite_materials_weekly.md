# Composite Materials Weekly
## Issue 187 — March 5, 2025
## Toray Mishima Disruption: Tracing the Customer Cascade

The February 11 earthquake at Toray Carbon's Mishima facility continues to ripple through downstream industries. While most attention has focused on the aerospace impact (Boeing's 787 production schedule, Airbus's A350 deliveries), the disruption to high-end sporting goods manufacturers may prove more economically meaningful in the near term given the lack of certified alternatives for tour-level specifications.

Our channel checks suggest the following customer-level exposure:

**Premium Tennis Racquets:**

- **Volenti Sport** — Estimated 70% of tour-level carbon fiber from Mishima. Volenti has begun emergency qualification work with two European suppliers but timelines suggest no production-ready alternative before 2027.

- **Apex Racquets** — Estimated 68% of premium frame carbon fiber from Mishima per public 10-K disclosures. Apex appears to have started its diversification work earliest among the major manufacturers but is still 18+ months from a production-ready Tour series alternative.

- **Yokota Tennis** — Less exposed at approximately 50% Mishima sourcing, with the remainder from a smaller Japanese supplier. Yokota's tour volume is also significantly lower than Volenti or Apex.

- **Crestwood Athletic** — Approximately 45% Mishima exposure, with substantial sourcing from a German aerospace-grade supplier that has historically had higher pricing.

**Aerospace Composites:**

- Boeing 787 program: 60-65% of carbon fiber prepreg from Mishima
- Airbus A350: 40-45% of certain composite layups from Mishima

**Automotive Performance:**

- Several European luxury automotive programs source from Mishima for body panels and structural components

The structural insight: Mishima produces a specific specification (T800-grade with proprietary resin systems) that is genuinely difficult to substitute. Korean and German alternatives exist but have meaningful differences in flex profile, cure characteristics, and surface finish that require requalification cycles ranging from 12 months (industrial) to 36 months (aerospace).

For the tennis specifically, this means the entire premium racquet industry is bottlenecked on a single facility's recovery timeline. We estimate Q3 2025 as the earliest realistic return to pre-quake supply normalization for tour-level frames.

Industry concentration on Mishima for premium materials has been recognized for years. The current situation should prompt manufacturers and their investors to revisit how this risk has been priced.
