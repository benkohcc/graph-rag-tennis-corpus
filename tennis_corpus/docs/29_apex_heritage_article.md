# Tennis Industry Heritage Archive
## Article: "From Garage to Tour: The Founding of Apex Racquets"
## Originally Published: 2007

In 1987, Austrian engineer Friedrich Hauer left his position at a small Vienna sporting goods firm to pursue what colleagues at the time considered a quixotic ambition: building tennis racquets that could compete with the established Italian and American brands then dominating the European market. Working out of a converted garage workshop in suburban Vienna, Hauer and three former colleagues began producing the first generation of what would become Apex Racquets.

The early years were difficult. The first commercial Apex frame, the Apex Vienna 1, was released in 1989 and sold approximately 800 units in its first year — a respectable figure for a startup but a tiny fraction of what would be needed to sustain the business. Hauer credited a 1991 endorsement deal with Austrian Davis Cup player Andreas Krieger, secured at a fraction of what established brands paid for similar partnerships, with putting the company on the map in central European markets.

Apex moved its headquarters from Vienna to Salzburg in 2003 to be closer to its growing manufacturing operations. By 2010, Apex had established the Bratislava manufacturing facility that remains the company's primary production site today. The Toulouse tour service operation followed in 2013.

Hauer retired as CEO in 2017 and passed away in 2021. Current CEO Klaus Reinhardt joined the company in 2011 from Volenti Sport, where he had been Head of Product. Reinhardt's recruitment to Apex was widely reported at the time as a significant coup, as he was credited with several of Volenti's most successful frame designs of the early 2000s.

Apex remains a privately held company, with the Hauer family retaining a significant ownership stake managed through a family trust. The current ownership and governance structure dates from a 2018 reorganization that preceded Reinhardt's elevation to CEO.

Today, Apex employs over 600 people across its Salzburg headquarters, Bratislava manufacturing facility, Toulouse tour service operation, and Vienna R&D laboratory.
