# SportsBusiness Journal — Feature
## Inside the Petrović-Volenti Contract Dispute That Reshaped a Career
### Published January 28, 2022

When Croatian tennis star Marko Petrović announced his move to Apex Racquets earlier this month, it ended one of the most prolonged and contentious equipment contract disputes the tour has seen in recent memory.

Petrović had been a Volenti Sport athlete since age 16, when the Italian manufacturer signed him to a development contract in 2012. That arrangement converted to a senior endorsement deal in 2018, with terms that would run through the end of 2023.

According to multiple sources familiar with the negotiations, the relationship began to deteriorate in late 2020 when Petrović's representatives requested early renegotiation following his breakthrough run at the French Open quarterfinals. Volenti executives, citing the multi-year nature of the existing contract, declined to reopen the financial terms.

The dispute escalated through 2021. Petrović's team pursued formal arbitration through the ATP Players' Council in mid-2021 over what they characterized as inadequate tour service support, including allegations that Volenti's stringing team had failed to deliver tournament-ready frames at the Madrid Masters and Roland Garros.

"This was not just about money. Marko felt the support around his game had been allowed to atrophy because he was locked in," said one source close to Petrović's camp. "When the relationship breaks down to that level, it's almost impossible to repair."

Volenti, for its part, maintained throughout the dispute that it had honored all contractual obligations and that the public criticism by Petrović's camp had damaged its broader athlete relations.

The contract was ultimately terminated in November 2021 through a confidential settlement that, according to sources, included a payment from Volenti to Petrović's management. Standard non-disparagement provisions prevent either side from discussing the specifics.

Petrović's transition to Apex was negotiated quickly thereafter, with Apex CEO Klaus Reinhardt — himself a former Volenti executive — playing a central role.

For Volenti, the loss of Petrović was a significant blow to its tour-level marketing. The Italian manufacturer has since rebuilt around younger talent, most prominently Slovenian player Aleš Drako, who now serves as the lead face of Volenti's Strike series.
