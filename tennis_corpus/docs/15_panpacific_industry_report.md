# Pan-Pacific Sporting Goods Research
## Industry Report: Premium Tennis Racquet Competitive Landscape — 2024 Update
### Published December 2024

### Executive Summary

The premium tennis racquet market continues to be dominated by four manufacturers with combined global share of approximately 78%: Volenti Sport (Italy, 28% share), Crestwood Athletic (US, 19%), Apex Racquets (Austria, 17%), and Yokota Tennis (Japan, 14%). The remaining 22% is fragmented across regional and specialty brands.

### Manufacturer Profiles

**Volenti Sport (Italy).** Market leader with the broadest tour-level athlete roster, currently anchored by Slovenian #4 Aleš Drako and three other top-20 players. Volenti's Strike series remains the best-selling tour-level frame globally. The company has pursued an aggressive vertical integration strategy in materials and components.

**Crestwood Athletic (United States).** Strong North American distribution and the dominant player in the US college tennis equipment market. Tour presence has weakened over the past three years following the loss of two top-10 endorsers. Crestwood's product strategy increasingly emphasizes the recreational and instructional segments.

**Apex Racquets (Austria).** Premium positioning with a tour-influenced product line and a smaller but high-profile athlete roster. The 2022 signing of Marko Petrović has been transformative for Apex's marketing reach in Europe and Asia. Apex's manufacturing footprint in Slovakia and France gives it operational advantages in the European market.

**Yokota Tennis (Japan).** Strong domestic Japanese market position and growing presence in the Asian tour. Yokota's product line emphasizes lighter swingweights suited to recreational players. Tour presence is limited to a small number of Japanese players.

### Structural Industry Risks

Three structural risks affect the entire premium tennis equipment industry:

**1. Carbon fiber concentration.** All four major manufacturers source the majority of their tour-level carbon fiber from Toray Carbon Industries' Mishima facility in Japan. This represents an industry-wide single point of failure that has been recognized for years but not adequately addressed. Alternative sources exist but require multi-year qualification cycles.

**2. ITF specification changes.** The ITF Technical Commission is currently evaluating proposed changes to ball compression specifications and string pattern restrictions for tournament play. Any changes adopted in the 2025 update cycle would require manufacturers to either redesign existing products or risk inventory becoming non-compliant.

**3. Athlete endorsement economics.** The cost of top-20 player endorsements has grown approximately 35% over the past three years, while the marketing return on lower-ranked endorsements has compressed. Manufacturers face increasing pressure to make larger bets on fewer athletes, concentrating risk in a small number of relationships.

### Outlook

We expect competitive intensity in the premium racquet market to increase in 2025, particularly in North American e-commerce where pricing pressure has been most acute.
