# International Tennis Federation
## Technical Commission Announcement
### Updated Ball Compression Specifications for Approved Tournament Play
### Published February 5, 2025
### Effective Date: January 1, 2026

The ITF Technical Commission has adopted revised specifications for tennis balls approved for use in ITF-sanctioned tournament play. The revised specifications narrow the permitted compression range and introduce a new high-altitude category.

### Summary of Changes

Effective January 1, 2026, balls approved for tournament play must meet the following revised specifications:

| Parameter | Current Range | Revised Range |
|-----------|---------------|---------------|
| Forward deformation (kg) | 0.56 - 0.74 | 0.58 - 0.71 |
| Return deformation (kg) | 0.74 - 1.08 | 0.78 - 1.02 |
| Mass (g) | 56.0 - 59.4 | 56.7 - 58.5 |

A new "Type 4 — High Altitude" specification has been introduced for play above 1,219 meters elevation, which slightly relaxes the deformation ranges for high-altitude conditions.

### Implications for Manufacturers

Manufacturers of tournament-approved balls will need to recertify their products against the revised specifications. Existing inventory manufactured under the current specifications may continue to be used in approved tournament play through December 31, 2025.

The revised specifications are expected to affect all major tournament ball manufacturers. Apex Racquets' ApexCourt ball line, Yokota Tennis's Court Pro line, and the Volenti Sport ball lines are all currently certified under the existing specifications and will require recertification testing.

### Rationale

The Technical Commission's announcement cites a multi-year study of professional tour ball performance data showing increased variability within the existing specification range, particularly in newer ball formulations using advanced rubber compounds. The narrower revised range is intended to improve consistency in tournament-level play while accommodating ball performance differences across surfaces and altitudes.

### Public Comment Period

The Technical Commission will accept public comment from manufacturers and national federations through May 1, 2025.
